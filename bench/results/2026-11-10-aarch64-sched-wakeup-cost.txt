Host
----
arch          aarch64 (ARM), 2 cores (Parallels VM)
kernel        7.0.0-43-generic
ptop          feat/151-runq-after-wakeup against main (73f09cf), eBPF build (-tags=ebpf)
target        an idle `sleep 600`: its threads take no part in the load
load          a non-target ping-pong: two locked threads bounce a byte over two
              pipes, each blocking in read until the other writes
command       sudo probecost -window 10s, while ptop --pid <sleep> --serve ran

What #151 adds is a program on sched:sched_wakeup, which runs for EVERY wakeup
on the host and cannot be filtered before it runs: the event fires in the
waker's context, so the only identity it carries is the woken task's pid, and
the program looks that up in root2ns to learn whether it is the target's. The
load is chosen to make that lookup miss as often as possible, as it does on a
real host where nearly every wakeup belongs to someone else.

Three rounds, each the ptop from main and then this branch, ten seconds of
probecost apiece. Only the sched programs are shown; the rest are unchanged.

Reading
-------
- handle_sched_wakeup costs 55-59 ns a wakeup, nearly all of it the root2ns
  miss. At this host's ~120k wakeups a second that is 0.7% of a core; it scales
  with the host's wakeup rate, not the target's: ~0.6% of a core per 100k
  wakeups a second, ~6% at a million.
- The two sched_switch programs (cpu and threads) are unchanged within noise:
  the threads one gained a branch for the histogram, on the switch-ins of the
  target's own threads only.
- The ping-pong's own throughput (74k-114k round trips a second with either
  ptop, 122k with none) moved more between rounds than between builds, so it
  says nothing about the difference. The kernel's run-time accounting does.
- The runs/s of handle_sched_wakeup is about half of sched_switch's: a pipe
  read that finds its byte already there wakes nobody.
