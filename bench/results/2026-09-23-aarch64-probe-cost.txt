This run exists because `make bench` could not answer the question #125 raised,
and could never have. That harness measures a probe by the increase it causes
in the target's CPU time per unit of work, and its floor is this host's noise —
±13% to ±35% across three attempts here, ±1.8% at the best this repository has
ever recorded. The CPU attribution sampler costs about a hundredth of one
percent of a core. Two orders of magnitude separate the effect from the ruler,
so the attempts produced negative overheads and no answer.

Reading it
----------

handle_cpu_samp is the #125 sampler: 198 runs per second, which is exactly the
requested 99Hz times this host's two CPUs, at 279ns per run, for 0.006% of a
core. An earlier ad-hoc measurement of the same build put it at 533ns/run and
0.011%; the per-run figure moves with cache state between runs, the order of
magnitude does not.

It is not the most expensive probe and it is not close. The syscall tracepoints
cost six times more between them, purely because they fire sixty times as
often — 11.5k/s against 198/s — while costing a tenth as much per firing. That
is the shape of the whole table: what a probe costs is dominated by how often
the target makes it fire, not by what it does when it does.

The heap uprobe does not appear because this workload allocates almost nothing,
so it never fired. That is the probe `make bench` exists for, and the one this
tool is worst at: its cost lives entirely in how often the target allocates,
which is a property of the target, not of the probe.

Caveats
-------

  - These count time INSIDE each program. The trap, interrupt and tracepoint
    machinery that reaches the program is not counted, nor is the cache and
    branch-predictor disturbance left behind for the target. Each figure is a
    floor on the true cost.
  - Enabling the statistics costs a timestamp pair per run, charged to the
    program, so the numbers overstate slightly.
  - The kernel truncates program names to 15 characters, which is why two
    sched_switch handlers both read "handle_sched_sw". They are separate rows:
    the tool keys on program id, not on name.
